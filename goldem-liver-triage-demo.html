Liver lab triage, a working sample

A small neural network trained on 608 real, anonymised patients from a public dataset reads one blood panel and ranks four outcomes: healthy blood donor, hepatitis C, fibrosis or cirrhosis. Change any value and the result updates in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

Research sample on the public HCV dataset (UCI, CC BY 4.0). Not a medical device and not for clinical use.

The full system adds imaging where the labs run out

Hepatitis, fatty liver, fibrosis, cirrhosis and cancer leave different traces: some in blood, some only in ultrasound, CT or MRI. The design below lets each kind of data do what it does best and keeps a clinician in charge of the final call.

1. IntakeSymptoms, history, risk factors and labs as structured forms; images uploaded or pulled from the hospital PACS.
2. Clinical modelGradient-boosted trees on labs and history, with FIB-4 and APRI as fixed reference scores.
3. Imaging modelsCNNs for ultrasound (steatosis grade), CT and MRI (liver and lesion segmentation, nodule classification).
4. FusionCombines the outputs into one ranked differential with calibrated probabilities.
5. Clinician reviewHeat maps over the image and the lab values that drove the result; the doctor confirms or corrects, and that is logged.

Models and public data to start from

  • Lab and history model: gradient boosting (XGBoost or LightGBM) with SHAP explanations; public starting data ILPD and HCV (UCI).
  • CT liver and tumour segmentation: nnU-Net style 3D U-Net, trained on LiTS (131 scans) and Medical Segmentation Decathlon Task 03.
  • CT and MRI organ segmentation: CHAOS challenge data.
  • Ultrasound fatty liver grading: transfer learning (EfficientNet or ResNet, TensorFlow/Keras) on published B-mode datasets, then the client's own images.
  • Cancer lesion classification (for example LI-RADS categories) only once enough labelled local cases exist.

Validation before anyone relies on it

  • Split by patient and by hospital, never by image, so no patient leaks between training and testing.
  • External test on a site the model has never seen; report sensitivity, specificity, AUC with confidence intervals and calibration.
  • Compare against FIB-4, APRI and the radiologist's report, then a reader study: clinicians with and without the tool.
  • Silent pilot: the system runs beside real cases without showing results, until the numbers hold.

Technology

  • AI service in Python: TensorFlow/Keras for imaging, scikit-learn and XGBoost for tabular models, served behind a REST API (FastAPI).
  • Images: DICOM handled with an Orthanc server and shown in the OHIF web viewer, so doctors see the overlay on the real scan.
  • Web app for clinicians: React, with role-based access, audit log of every prediction and every correction.
  • Data: PostgreSQL for records, object storage for images, encryption at rest and in transit, de-identification before training.
  • Every model versioned with its training data and metrics (MLflow), so any result can be traced to the exact model that produced it.
